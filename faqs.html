Most Asked

Fitment Data Management

QWhat is fitment data and why does it matter so much for auto parts ecommerce?

Fitment data is the Year/Make/Model (and sometimes Engine) information that tells a customer whether a part will work on their specific vehicle. Without accurate fitment data, customers can't find your products in filtered search, Google Shopping can't serve them to the right buyer, and you end up with high return rates because people ordered the wrong part.

Most platforms - Shopify, BigCommerce, and the major search engines - all use fitment structured data to power vehicle-specific search filters. If your fitment data is missing, incomplete, or formatted inconsistently, your products simply won't appear when someone searches for parts for their truck.

QWhat do I do when my supplier's fitment data is incomplete or inconsistent?

This is the most common problem we see. Supplier data ranges from clean VCDB-formatted files to messy spreadsheets with missing submodels, nonstandard engine names, or gaps for entire product lines.

The fix depends on the scale of the problem:

  • •Small gaps: Manual correction with a defined format standard you enforce going forward.
  • •Systematic supplier problems: We build a normalization layer that maps supplier fitment values to VCDB (Vehicle Configuration Database) standard - so even if the supplier says "6.6L Duramax," the system knows what years and submodels that covers.
  • •Missing data entirely: Products with no fitment get flagged and either excluded from filtered search or tagged as "universal fit" if that's accurate.
Rule of thumb: a product with bad fitment data is worse than a product with no fitment data. Bad data sends customers to the wrong part; missing data just means they don't find you.

QShould I use Year/Make/Model or Year/Make/Engine for my fitment search?

It depends on your catalog. Year/Make/Model (YMM) is the standard and works well for most exterior accessories, body parts, and universal upgrades. Year/Make/Engine (YME) is necessary when the part's fitment is engine-specific - diesel performance parts, fuel injectors, turbos, emissions equipment.

Many catalogs need both: a customer searching for a cold air intake for a 2018 Ram 2500 needs to specify whether they have the 6.4L Hemi or the 6.7L Cummins before you can show the right part. If you serve diesel performance specifically, engine-level fitment isn't optional.

QWhat is a PIM and do I need one?

A PIM (Product Information Manager) is a central system for managing all your product data - titles, descriptions, specs, images, fitment, pricing - before it gets pushed to your storefront, your feeds, and any other channels.

You probably need one if: you have more than 5,000 SKUs, you sell across multiple channels (site + eBay + Amazon + feeds), your team is managing product data in a shared Google Sheet, or you're getting products from multiple suppliers with different data formats.

Without a PIM, every channel update becomes a manual project and data quality degrades over time. With one, you fix a product record once and it propagates everywhere.

QWhat's the right way to handle "universal fit" parts in a YMM-filtered catalog?

Universal parts are tricky. If you tag them with YMM fitment, they'll surface in vehicle-specific searches - which is good for discovery but can confuse customers who assume the part is vehicle-specific. If you leave them untagged, they disappear from filtered search entirely.

The best approach: tag universal parts with a "Universal" fitment flag (Shopify supports this natively), and make sure your product page prominently states that the part is not vehicle-specific. This keeps them visible in search while setting correct customer expectations.

Most Confusing

Ad Performance & Attribution

QWhy do my Facebook ad numbers look completely different from Google Analytics?

Because they're measuring different things with different rules - and Facebook is almost always going to show you a bigger number.

Facebook's default attribution window counts a purchase as a "conversion" if a customer saw your ad (even for one second without clicking it) and then bought anything from your store within 30 days. Google Analytics only counts it if the customer clicked your ad and bought within the session or a short window.

The result: if someone sees your Facebook ad on Monday, ignores it, Googles your product name on Friday, and buys - Facebook claims the sale, Google Analytics gives it to organic search, and your paid search data gets none of the credit. The real answer is usually somewhere in between.

We recommend switching Facebook attribution to 7-day click-through only. You'll see fewer "conversions" reported, but the number will reflect reality much more closely.

QWhat is a good ROAS for an automotive parts store?

ROAS (Return on Ad Spend) by itself isn't a reliable benchmark - it depends entirely on your margins. A high-margin accessories retailer might be profitable at 4:1 ROAS; a low-margin parts distributor might need 15:1 to break even.

A more useful metric is your ad spend as a percentage of total revenue. For most automotive ecommerce retailers, 5-10% is a healthy range. Under 5% often means you're leaving growth on the table; over 15% usually signals that ads are propping up a conversion or margin problem that needs to be fixed at the site level.

QMy revenue dropped but my ROAS stayed high. What's going on?

ROAS is a ratio - it can stay high even while actual revenue falls if your ad spend also dropped proportionally. This is one of the most common misreadings we see.

Other causes: your average order value (AOV) declined (customers buying cheaper parts), your traffic mix shifted toward lower-intent visitors, or a competitor is now taking more of the purchase-ready traffic that used to convert easily for you.

Always look at absolute revenue, AOV, and conversion rate together. ROAS in isolation doesn't tell you if the business is actually growing.

QWhy does Google Ads report more clicks than I see in Google Analytics?

This is normal and expected. Google Ads counts every click on your ad. Google Analytics only records a session if the click fires the tracking code successfully - which can fail due to slow page loads, ad blockers, users closing the page immediately, or tagging errors.

A 5-15% discrepancy is typical. If you're seeing 30-50% gaps, there's usually a tracking setup issue - often a missing or misfiring GTM tag - that's worth investigating.

For paid search performance analysis, trust Google Ads for click and cost data. Trust Google Analytics for on-site behavior and multi-channel attribution.

QHow do I know when it's time to increase my ad budget?

Three signals that you have room to spend more profitably: your impression share is below 60% on your best-performing search campaigns (meaning you're losing auctions you could win), your shopping feed has products with zero impressions that are correctly set up and competitive, or your ad spend-to-revenue ratio has been consistently below 7% for 60+ days.

Don't increase budget to fix a performance problem - fix the problem first. Spending more on a campaign with poor conversion rates just accelerates the loss.

Operations

ERP & Shopify Integration

QCan Shopify integrate with my ERP?

Yes - with the right middleware. Shopify's native API connects to ERPs like NetSuite, Acumatica, Epicor, and MAM-based systems, but these integrations rarely come out of the box. You need a connector layer that maps your ERP's data model to Shopify's.

The questions that determine complexity: Does your ERP have a modern REST API or does it require flat-file exports? Do you need real-time sync or is a scheduled batch update acceptable? Are you syncing one-way (ERP to Shopify) or two-way (orders from Shopify back into ERP)?

QHow do orders flow from my website into my parts management system?

The standard flow: customer places order on Shopify, order webhook fires to your integration layer, order is created in your ERP/parts management system (e.g., MAM, Tangle, NetSuite), ERP handles picking, packing, and shipping, tracking number is pushed back to Shopify, customer gets automated shipping notification.

The failure point is almost always the return path: getting tracking numbers back into Shopify reliably and fast enough that customers aren't emailing asking where their order is. Build and test that leg carefully.

QWhat does "two-way sync" mean and do I actually need it?

Two-way sync means data flows in both directions: Shopify sends orders to your ERP, and your ERP sends inventory levels (and optionally prices) back to Shopify. Most serious automotive parts retailers need it.

Without it, you're either manually updating stock on your site after every warehouse move, or you're overselling products you don't have. Both are expensive in customer service time and refunds.

If you're processing more than 20-30 orders per day manually, you're already past the point where two-way sync pays for itself in the first month.

QWe're evaluating ERPs right now. Which one should we choose?

We've worked integrations with NetSuite, Acumatica, Epicor, Tangle, and MAM-based systems. Here's the honest breakdown for automotive parts retailers:

  • •NetSuite: Most powerful, most expensive, biggest implementation lift. Good if you're doing $10M+ in revenue and need deep financial consolidation.
  • •Acumatica: Strong middle ground - good Shopify connector ecosystem, automotive industry modules available, more accessible implementation cost.
  • •Tangle: Built specifically for automotive parts distribution. Understands fitment, cores, and the wholesale/retail split natively. Shopify integration is available.
  • •MAM: Common in the UK/European market, but used by some US operations. Integration requires custom API work.

Whatever you choose: confirm the Shopify connector exists before you sign a contract, and budget 3-6 months for a realistic go-live timeline.

QHow long does ERP integration typically take?

For a standard order sync integration with an ERP that has a documented API: 6-12 weeks to go live, assuming you have a dedicated resource on your side to configure and test the ERP side. Add 4-8 weeks if you're also migrating historical data or building out inventory sync simultaneously.

The biggest delay is almost always access and credentials - we can't build what we can't connect to. Have your IT team or ERP vendor ready on day one.

Feeds & Ads

Google Shopping Feeds

QWhy did my Google Shopping traffic suddenly drop?

The most common causes, in order of how often we see them:

  • •Robots.txt blocking variant URLs - If your product variants (size, color, fitment) are on separate URLs and robots.txt blocks them, Google can't crawl or serve those pages. A single line in robots.txt can wipe out a significant portion of your catalog from Shopping.
  • •Feed disapprovals - Google Merchant Center will flag and disapprove products for missing GTINs, incorrect pricing, image quality issues, or policy violations. Check the Diagnostics tab.
  • •Collection reindexing lag - After major collection or metafield changes on Shopify, the feed can take 24-72 hours to reflect updates. Products may temporarily show wrong data or disappear.
  • •Condition field misuse - Labeling products as "refurbished" when they're new, or leaving condition blank, can cause disapprovals or badge products as "used" in Shopping - killing conversion rate even when the ad still shows.

QWhat product data is actually required for Google Shopping to work properly?

Required fields: id, title, description, link, image_link, availability, price, brand, and condition. For auto parts specifically, Google also requires gtin (or mpn + brand if no GTIN exists) and strongly recommends vehicle_compatibility data for fitment filtering.

Where most auto parts feeds break down: titles that are too generic ("Oil Filter" instead of "WIX 51085 Oil Filter for 2015-2022 Ford F-150 5.0L"), missing or wrong GTINs, and descriptions that are just a copy of the title.

Google's algorithm uses your title to match your product to search queries. A well-structured title (Brand + Part Number + Part Type + Vehicle Fitment) can double your impressions with no change to bids.

QCan labeling a part as "remanufactured" or "used" hurt my sales?

Yes - significantly. Google Shopping surfaces a "Refurbished" or "Used" badge on product cards when the condition field is set to anything other than "new." Even if you're selling a legitimate, warrantied remanufactured part, many buyers will skip it when they see that badge.

The right approach: check what your supplier actually means by "remanufactured." Many aftermarket parts described as reman by the supplier are technically new in terms of the final product (new core, new components, new warranty). In those cases, "new" is often the accurate condition classification. When in doubt, consult Google's condition policy before assuming you need to flag it.

QWhy do my products disappear from Shopping after I make collection changes?

Shopify's feed apps and Google's crawl cycle don't always update in sync. When you restructure collections, change metafields, or update product tags, the feed app may serve stale data for up to 24-48 hours, and Google's cache takes additional time to update on top of that.

For major catalog restructuring: make changes in batches, manually trigger a feed re-fetch in Merchant Center after each batch, and monitor Diagnostics for the next 72 hours. Don't wait for things to self-resolve if you're seeing disapproval spikes - act on them the same day.

Site Experience

Inventory Display Strategy

QShould I show customers exact inventory quantities or just "In Stock / Out of Stock"?

For most automotive parts retailers: show availability status, not quantities.

Showing exact quantities creates problems: it reveals your stocking strategy to competitors, it can make a product with only 2 units feel risky to buy even though it'll ship today, and it requires your inventory data to be hyper-accurate in real time (which it usually isn't, once you factor in orders being processed, returns in transit, and vendor discrepancies).

A clear "In Stock - Ships Today" message with a fulfillment timeline converts better than a quantity number in most parts categories.

QWhat's the best approach for out-of-stock products - hide them or show a message?

Don't hide them. Out-of-stock product pages accumulate SEO equity, backlinks, and PPC landing page history. Removing them kills all of that and creates 404 errors if anyone has the URL bookmarked or shared.

Instead: show a custom availability message ("Currently out of stock - email us for ETA" or "Available to order - ships in 5-7 days"), add a "notify me when available" email capture, and if you can still sell it as a pre-order or special order, make that path clear. The goal is to keep the page alive and give the customer a next action.

QHow do I message availability differently for in-house stock vs. drop-ship?

Customers don't need to know the logistics behind it, but they do need accurate timing. The distinction matters operationally for you:

  • •In-house: "In Stock - Ships same day if ordered before 2 PM EST"
  • •Drop-ship: "Ships from manufacturer in 2-4 business days" or a brand-specific message like "Ships directly from Turn 14 Distribution"
  • •Special order: "Available by request - contact us for lead time"

The key is accuracy. Overpromising on drop-ship lead times is the fastest way to generate customer service volume and negative reviews.

Organic

SEO for Auto Parts Ecommerce

QWhat does a technical SEO audit actually look at for an automotive ecommerce site?

For auto parts specifically, we focus on: duplicate meta titles and descriptions across product variants, internal broken links (common after catalog restructuring), crawl budget waste from parameter URLs and infinite scroll, structured data (especially vehicle fitment schema), page speed on mobile (critical for parts shoppers searching from the shop floor), and robots.txt/noindex misconfigurations that block your products from being indexed.

We also look at your URL structure - whether collection and product URLs are consistent, canonical tags are correct, and pagination is handled properly. These issues are invisible to the naked eye but compound over months into meaningful ranking losses.

QHow do duplicate product titles and descriptions hurt my search rankings?

Google's algorithm struggles to differentiate between pages with identical or near-identical titles and descriptions. When it can't tell your pages apart, it picks one to rank and deprioritizes the rest - often not the one you'd choose.

For parts catalogs, this happens most with variant pages (same part in multiple finishes or sizes) and with supplier-provided descriptions that dozens of other retailers are also using verbatim. Unique titles with part number + fitment + brand, and unique descriptions that go beyond the spec sheet, give each page a reason to exist in Google's index.

QWhat is server-side tracking and do I need it?

Standard Google Analytics and ad pixel tracking runs in the browser (client-side). If a customer has an ad blocker, a privacy browser setting, or a slow connection that causes the script to not fully load, that conversion doesn't get counted. For some stores, 20-30% of actual purchases are going untracked this way.

Server-side tracking moves the tracking to your server - so the conversion event fires regardless of what's happening in the customer's browser. The result is more accurate conversion data, better algorithm optimization for your paid campaigns, and less signal loss for remarketing audiences.

If you're spending more than $5,000/month on paid ads, the data quality improvement from server-side tracking typically pays back in better campaign performance within 60-90 days.

QHow do I prioritize which product pages to optimize for SEO first?

Start with your Search Terms report in Google Ads and Google Search Console. Find the terms driving the most clicks and impressions where your pages are ranking 5-20 (page one, bottom half, or page two) - those are your highest-leverage opportunities. A page ranked #12 that gets small improvements to title, content, and internal linking can move to #4-6 and double or triple clicks with no ad spend increase.

Secondary priority: your highest-revenue product categories where you have inventory depth. A well-optimized category page that ranks for "leveling kits for F-250" is worth more long-term than 50 individually optimized product pages.

Paid Social

Meta (Facebook & Instagram) Ads

QDo Facebook and Instagram ads actually work for automotive parts retailers?

Yes - with realistic expectations about what they're good at. Meta is not a replacement for Google Shopping or paid search. Customers don't go to Facebook to search for a specific part they need today. What Meta is excellent at: reaching truck enthusiasts and off-road communities with lifestyle content, driving awareness for new products or brands, retargeting past visitors and customers, and building lookalike audiences from your customer list.

The clients we see Meta work best for are those selling accessories and upgrades (lift kits, wheels, exhausts, lighting) to enthusiast communities where brand and lifestyle identity matter. For commodity replacement parts purchased on price, Meta ROI is usually weaker than search.

QWho should own the Facebook Ads Manager account - us or the agency?

You should always own the ad account. Full stop. Your ad account contains your pixel data, your audience history, your conversion data, and your billing relationship with Meta. If you ever change agencies, you need to be able to walk away with all of that intact.

The right setup: you create and own the Business Manager and Ad Account, then grant your agency Partner access. We can operate everything within your account without needing ownership. Never let an agency create the ad account in their own Business Manager on your behalf - getting that data back if the relationship ends is a painful process.

QHow do you build a lookalike audience for diesel truck owners or off-road enthusiasts?

The best lookalike audiences start from your own customer data - a list of people who actually bought from you. Upload your customer email list to Meta, create a Custom Audience, and then build a Lookalike Audience from it. Meta finds people with similar behavior and demographic patterns to your real buyers.

For diesel performance specifically, we supplement with interest-based layers: Duramax, Powerstroke, Ram Cummins, diesel performance brands (Banks Power, Fleece Performance, etc.), and truck-specific Facebook groups and pages. The combination of a lookalike base plus interest layering tends to outperform either approach alone.

The smaller and more specific your source audience, the more precise your lookalike will be. A list of 500 repeat customers will outperform a list of 50,000 one-time buyers.

QHow many fields should I ask for on a Facebook lead gen form?

As few as possible - typically just name and email for a first-touch campaign. Every additional field (phone, vehicle year, make, model) reduces completion rate, often dramatically. The exception is if you're running a "get a quote" or "check fit" campaign where the additional fields are directly tied to the value you're offering - customers will provide vehicle info if they need it to get an answer.

Get the conversion first. Collect additional data via post-signup email flows once the relationship is established.

Multichannel

eBay & Multi-Channel Selling

QCan I sync my Shopify inventory to eBay automatically?

Yes. Apps like Codisto, Trunk, and Shopify's own eBay sales channel connect your Shopify product catalog to eBay listings and keep inventory levels in sync. When a product sells on Shopify, the eBay listing quantity decrements automatically, and vice versa.

The setup is straightforward for simple products. Complexity increases with fitment data, product variants, and if you're managing multiple eBay stores or seller accounts - that requires either a multi-store-capable app or custom integration work.

QDoes my fitment data transfer from Shopify to eBay?

Not automatically. eBay uses its own Parts Compatibility system (based on eBay's vehicle catalog, not VCDB) that requires separate mapping. Fitment data structured correctly for Shopify/Google doesn't push directly to eBay compatibility tables.

You have two options: manually add compatibility data in eBay Seller Hub for high-priority listings, or build an automated mapping layer that translates your VCDB-formatted fitment into eBay's compatibility format. If eBay is a meaningful revenue channel for you, the mapping investment pays back quickly in better listing visibility and reduced "does this fit my truck" messages.

QHow do I manage inventory across multiple eBay stores from one place?

You need either a multi-channel inventory management platform (Linnworks, Skuvault, or ChannelAdvisor at scale) or a custom integration that treats your Shopify or ERP as the inventory master and pushes updates to each eBay account. The key requirement: one source of truth for available quantity that all channels draw from simultaneously.

Without a single source of truth, you'll oversell. It's not a question of if - it's when.

Pricing

MAP Pricing & Promotions

QHow do I run promotions without violating MAP policies?

MAP (Minimum Advertised Price) restricts what price you can advertise publicly - but it typically doesn't restrict what you can charge after a customer initiates checkout or applies a coupon. The key distinction is "advertised" vs. "sold at."

Common compliant approaches:

  • •Cart-level discounts: Display MAP price on the product page; apply a discount at checkout. The advertised price is MAP-compliant; the transaction price can be lower.
  • •Coupon codes with minimum order values: The coupon is applied to the order total, not the individual item - many MAP policies allow this.
  • •"Click to see price" or "Add to cart to see price": Some brands allow this approach, where the discounted price isn't publicly visible until the customer takes an action.
MAP policies vary by brand and update frequently. Read each vendor's specific MAP policy before running any promotion - don't assume what works for one brand applies to another.

QShould I display MSRP and savings on my product pages?

Usually yes - when done correctly. Showing "MSRP: $149.99 | Your Price: $119.99 (Save $30)" creates a price anchor that reduces comparison shopping and makes the value clear at a glance. It's particularly effective for accessories and upgrades where customers aren't sure what the going rate should be.

The caveat: your MSRP has to be accurate. If you inflate it to manufacture a fake "savings" amount, it erodes trust with customers who research and often violates FTC guidelines. Use manufacturer-provided MSRP, and if a supplier doesn't provide MSRP, be conservative with any suggested retail markup you apply.

QWe run frequent sales and promos. Is that hurting us long-term?

It can be. When you run promotions regularly, customers learn to wait. They'll browse, add to cart, and hold off because they know a 15% off email is coming in two weeks. The result is lower baseline conversion rates and an increasing dependence on promotional periods to hit revenue targets - a cycle that's hard to break without a short-term revenue dip.

A healthier mix: reserve significant discounts for genuine clearance or seasonal events (Black Friday, end-of-season), and use value-add promotions the rest of the time (free shipping thresholds, free gift with purchase, bundled offers). These drive conversion without training customers to expect a price cut on everything.

Retention

Email Marketing

QWhat should I actually be sending to my email list?

Most automotive parts retailers under-invest in email and over-rely on promotional blasts. The content that drives long-term list value:

  • •New product arrivals: Especially for enthusiasts following specific brands. "The new Banks Power Derringer tuner is now in stock" gets clicks.
  • •Vehicle-specific fitment content: "Top 5 upgrades for your LML Duramax" reaches a specific audience and positions you as the authority, not just a storefront.
  • •How-to and installation guides: Reduces pre-purchase hesitation and post-purchase returns. Customers who understand what they're buying are better customers.
  • •Restocked inventory alerts: If you have customers who browsed an out-of-stock item, a restock email converts at very high rates.
  • •Promotions - used sparingly: Reserve price-cut emails for genuine events, not every communication.

QShould I include prices in my product emails?

It depends on your price stability. If your prices fluctuate frequently (due to MAP changes, supplier cost changes, or dynamic pricing), putting specific prices in emails creates customer service problems when they click through and see a different price.

A better approach for price-volatile catalogs: focus emails on the product, the fitment, and the value story - and let the click-through to your site show the current price. This also gives your site a chance to convert at whatever margin is appropriate today, rather than locking you into a price that may no longer be valid.

QWhat email automations should I set up first?

In order of ROI for automotive parts retailers:

  • •Abandoned cart: 3-email sequence at 1 hour, 24 hours, and 72 hours. The first email should be a simple reminder with no discount; add an incentive in the third email only if needed.
  • •Post-purchase / review request: Ask for a review 10-14 days after delivery. Parts reviews that mention the vehicle fitment ("Fits my 2020 F-250 perfectly") are SEO gold.
  • •Win-back: Customers who bought once and haven't returned in 6 months. A simple "here's what's new" email recovers a meaningful percentage.
  • •Welcome sequence: 2-3 emails to new subscribers explaining who you are, what makes you different, and what to expect from being on the list.

QHow do I measure whether email is actually driving sales?

Use a 5-day click attribution window in your email platform (Klaviyo, Omnisend, etc.) - not the default 5-day open attribution that most platforms use out of the box. Open-based attribution massively inflates email revenue because it credits any purchase made within 5 days of an email being opened, even if the customer never clicked the email and bought from a Google search instead.

Cross-reference your email platform's reported revenue with the "Email / Newsletter" channel in Google Analytics. They'll never match exactly, but if your email platform is claiming 10x what GA shows, your attribution window is too aggressive.

B2B

Wholesale & B2B Dealer Portals

QCan I sell to both retail customers and wholesale dealers from the same Shopify store?

Yes. Shopify's B2B features (available on Shopify Plus) and third-party apps like Wholesale Helper allow you to run retail and wholesale from a single storefront with separate pricing, checkout flows, and access controls. Dealers register, get approved, and then see their negotiated pricing when logged in - retail customers see standard pricing with no access to dealer rates.

The advantage of a single store: one catalog to maintain, one set of product pages to optimize, and a single inventory source. The complexity: dealer pricing tiers by brand, MAP compliance at the dealer level, and approval workflows all need to be configured carefully.

QHow do I set up different pricing tiers for dealers vs. retail customers?

On Shopify Plus: use the native B2B company and price list features. You can assign specific price lists to specific dealer accounts, set percentage discounts off MSRP by product category, and restrict certain brands or product lines to approved dealers only.

On standard Shopify: Wholesale Helper or similar apps replicate most of this functionality with a tag-based system. Dealers are tagged at account approval; pricing rules apply to tagged accounts automatically.

Either way, build your wholesale pricing structure before you launch the portal - retrofitting pricing tiers onto an existing product catalog with hundreds of brands is significantly more work than getting it right at setup.
